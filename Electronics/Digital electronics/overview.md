Digital electronics is a branch of electronics that deals with the design, analysis, and operation of circuits that process, store, and communicate information using discrete signals (represented as distinct binary states, typically 0 and 1 or LOW and HIGH voltage levels) rather than continuous analog waveforms.

Key Topics
• Number Systems: The foundation of digital data, including Binary (base-2), Octal, Decimal, Hexadecimal, Gray code, and Binary-Coded Decimal (BCD).
• Logic Gates: The basic building blocks of digital logic circuits. Primary gates include AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR, and XNOR, with NAND and NOR serving as universal gates.
• Boolean Algebra & Simplification: Mathematical rules and theorems (such as DeMorgan’s laws) used to analyze and simplify logic expressions, Karnaugh Maps (K-maps), and Quine-McCluskey methods.
• Combinational Circuits: Circuits where the output depends only on the current inputs at any given moment (e.g., adders, subtractors, multiplexers, demultiplexers, encoders, decoders, and magnitude comparators).
• Sequential Circuits: Circuits where the output depends on current inputs and past states, incorporating memory and timing elements. Key components include latches, flip-flops (SR, JK, D, T), registers, and counters.
• Memory and Programmable Logic: Storage architectures including RAM, ROM, Programmable Logic Arrays (PLAs), and Field Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs).
• Hardware Description Languages (HDLs): Modern design and simulation using languages like Verilog and VHDL to program and configure digital hardware.

Key Applications
• Computers and Computing Devices: Personal computers, servers, and calculators rely on digital processors and arithmetic logic units (ALUs).
• Microcontrollers and Microprocessors: Integrated circuits that act as the "brain" of embedded systems, consumer appliances, and industrial controllers.
• Telecommunications and Networking: Digital signal processing (DSP), data modulation, and line coding for cellular networks, fiber optics, and internet routers.
• Consumer Electronics: Smartphones, digital watches, smart TVs, and digital audio/video equipment.
• Industrial Automation & Control Systems: Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs), robotic systems, and digital instrumentation sensors
