A sensor is an input device that detects physical, chemical, or biological changes in the environment (such as light, heat, pressure, or motion) and converts them into a measurable electrical or digital signal.

Key Topics & Classifications
• Active vs. Passive Sensors:
	• Active (Generator-type): Generate an electrical signal directly from the physical input without needing an external power supply (e.g., thermocouples, photodiodes, piezoelectric sensors).
	• Passive (Modulator-type): Require an external power supply and modify that electrical current or voltage based on environmental changes (e.g., thermistors, strain gauges).
• Analog vs. Digital Outputs:
	• Analog Sensors: Provide a continuous output signal that changes smoothly with the physical quantity (e.g., standard temperature or pressure sensors).
	• Digital Sensors: Convert signals into discrete binary bits or bytes for direct communication with microprocessors.
• Common Sensor Types:
	• Temperature & Humidity Sensors: Measure thermal energy and moisture levels in the air or materials.
	• Pressure & Flow Sensors: Track force per unit area or fluid movement.
	• Motion & Proximity Sensors: Detect physical displacement, acceleration, or nearby objects without contact (e.g., accelerometers, LiDAR, parking sensors).
	• Optical & Light Sensors: Capture ambient light levels, infrared radiation, or image data (e.g., photodiodes, cameras).
	• Biosensors & Chemical Sensors: Combine biological or chemical recognition elements with electrical transducers to test glucose, pH, or gas concentrations.

Main Applications
• Consumer Electronics: Smartphones use ambient light sensors, accelerometers, gyroscopes, and capacitive touchscreens to manage UI control and orientation.
• Automotive Industry: Vehicles rely on pressure sensors (tire monitoring), LiDAR and proximity sensors (autonomous driving), and magnetic sensors (anti-lock braking).
• Healthcare & Wearables: Fitness trackers and medical devices monitor heart rate, blood oxygen levels, temperature, and physiological patterns.
• Industrial Automation & IoT: Smart factories and machine systems use continuous vibration and temperature monitoring for predictive maintenance and real-time data collection.
• Smart Cities & Environment: Urban networks deploy air quality monitors, acoustic seismic sensors, and smart waste or traffic sensors to optimize public infrastructure.
