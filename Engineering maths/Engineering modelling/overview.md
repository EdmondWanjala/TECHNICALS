Engineering modelling is the process of creating simplified mathematical, physical, or logical representations of real-world systems to analyze, predict, and optimize their behavior before physical fabrication.

    Key Topics in Engineering Modelling
* Dynamic Systems & Differential Equations: Representing time-varying physical behaviors using continuous-time or discrete-time mathematical formulations.
* Circuit & Network Theory: Modelling electrical properties using node and mesh equations, impedance, and transfer functions.
* Signal & Statistical Analysis: Describing random noise, signal transformations, and probability density functions for data processing.
* Finite State Machines (FSM) & Logic Models: Abstracting digital hardware states and sequential operations for computing systems.
* Simulation Tools: Utilizing software packages (like SPICE, MATLAB/Simulink, or Verilog/VHDL testbenches) to execute numerical models.

    Applications in Electronics and Computer Engineering
* Analog & Mixed-Signal Circuit Design: Simulating transistor-level responses, frequency filters, and amplifier linearity via SPICE models to prevent signal degradation.
* Control Systems & Robotics: Designing feedback loops for motor controllers or autonomous systems by modelling plant dynamics and stability criteria.
* Digital Logic & VLSI (Very Large Scale Integration): Modelling register-transfer levels (RTL) to verify microchip functionality and timing constraints before physical fabrication on silicon.
* Embedded Systems & IoT: Co-modelling hardware-software interactions to test microcontroller behavior under simulated sensor loads and power constraints
