1. ALGEBRAIC FOUNDATIONS

Algebra is the branch of mathematics that uses symbols, variables and mathematical operations to represent quantities and relationships. 
It provides the foundation for solving engineering equations, modelling physical systems and analysing mathematical relationships.

BASIC ALGEBRAIC CONCEPTS:
• Variable: A symbol representing an unknown or changing quantity (x, y, z).
• Constant: A fixed numerical value.
• Coefficient: A numerical factor multiplying a variable.
• Algebraic expression: A combination of variables, constants and operations.
• Equation: A mathematical statement asserting that two expressions are equal.
• Polynomial: An expression involving non-negative integer powers of variables.
• Degree: The highest power of the variable in a polynomial.
• Factorisation: Expressing an algebraic expression as a product of simpler factors.

Example:
3x² + 5x - 2
Degree = 2; coefficients = 3, 5 and -2.

ALGEBRAIC IDENTITIES:
Important identities used in expansion and factorisation:

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a - b)² = a² - 2ab + b²
(a + b)(a - b) = a² - b²
(a + b)³ = a³ + 3a²b + 3ab² + b³
(a - b)³ = a³ - 3a²b + 3ab² - b³
a³ + b³ = (a + b)(a² - ab + b²)
a³ - b³ = (a - b)(a² + ab + b²)

ALGEBRAIC FRACTIONS:
Algebraic fractions contain variables in their numerators or denominators.
Addition/subtraction:
    a/b ± c/d = (ad ± bc)/bd
Multiplication:
    (a/b)(c/d) = ac/bd
Division:
    (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = ad/bc
Always identify restrictions that make denominators zero and exclude those values from the domain.

ALGEBRAIC EQUATIONS:
Linear equation:
    ax + b = 0
    x = -b/a, where a ≠ 0.
Quadratic equation:
    ax² + bx + c = 0
Quadratic formula:
    x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a
Discriminant:
    Δ = b² - 4ac
• Δ > 0: Two distinct real roots.
• Δ = 0: Two equal real roots.
• Δ < 0: Two complex conjugate roots.
For roots α and β:
    α + β = -b/a
    αβ = c/a

INEQUALITIES:
Inequalities describe relationships involving greater than or less than.
Symbols:
> greater than
< less than
≥ greater than or equal to
≤ less than or equal to
Important rule:
Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses its direction.
Example:
-2x > 6
x < -3

EXPONENTIAL AND LOGARITHMIC EQUATIONS;
Exponential equations contain variables in exponents.
    If aˣ = aʸ, then x = y, provided a > 0 and a ≠ 1.
Logarithmic laws:
logₐ(xy) = logₐx + logₐy
logₐ(x/y) = logₐx - logₐy
logₐ(xⁿ) = n logₐx
logₐ1 = 0
logₐa = 1
Change of base:
    logₐx = log_bx / log_ba
Natural logarithm:
    ln(x) = logₑ(x)
Applications:
Exponential growth and decay, radioactive decay, capacitor charging/discharging, population models and signal analysis.

ENGINEERING TIP:
Always check the domain of logarithms (their arguments must be positive), denominators and square roots when solving equations.
