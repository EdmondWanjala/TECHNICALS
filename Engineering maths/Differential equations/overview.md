Differential equations are mathematical equations that relate a function to its derivatives, describing how a dynamic system changes over time or space.

    Overview and Classification
A differential equation contains one or more derivatives of an unknown function. They are split into two main types:
* Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs): Contain functions of a single independent variable (usually time \(t\)) and its ordinary derivatives.
* Partial Differential Equations (PDEs): Contain functions of multiple independent variables and their partial derivatives, modeling spatial and temporal variations.

    Key Topics
* First-Order DEs: Equations involving first derivatives, solved using techniques like separation of variables, integrating factors, or exact equations.
* Second-Order Linear DEs: Equations with second derivatives, categorized as homogeneous or non-homogeneous, solved via characteristic roots, undetermined coefficients, or variation of parameters.
* Laplace Transforms: An algebraic tool that converts complex time-domain differential equations into simpler frequency-domain algebraic equations to solve initial value problems.
* Systems of Differential Equations: Sets of multiple coupled DEs analyzed using matrices, eigenvalues, and phase planes.

    Applications in Electronics and Computer Engineering
* RLC Circuit Analysis: Kirchhoff's laws applied to resistors (\(R\)), inductors (\(L\)), and capacitors (\(C\)) yield first-order (\(RL\) or \(RC\)) and second-order (\(RLC\)) differential equations that model transient behavior, charging cycles, and natural frequencies.
* Filter and Amplifier Design: Operational amplifier circuits and analog filters use differential models to establish frequency and phase responses.
* Semiconductor Device Modeling: The behavior of charge carriers in diodes and transistors relies on diffusion-convection equations and non-linear differential relations.
* Transmission Lines and Signal Processing: High-speed computer buses and RF communication lines use the telegrapher's equations (coupled PDEs) to model signal attenuation, wave propagation, and impedance matching
