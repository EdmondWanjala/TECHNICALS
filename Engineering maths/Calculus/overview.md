Calculus is the mathematical study of continuous change, serving as the foundational language for modeling dynamic physical systems, signals, and computations.

    Overview of Calculus
* Core Concept: Measures how things change (differentiation) and accumulate (integration).
* Differential Calculus: Focuses on rates of change and slopes of curves using derivatives.
* Integral Calculus: Focuses on accumulation of quantities and areas under curves using integrals.
* Differential Equations: Connects functions with their rates of change to predict system behavior over time.

     Key Topics
* Limits and Continuity: The bedrock framework defining how functions behave as inputs approach specific values.
* Derivatives: Instantaneous rates of change (dy/dx), partial derivatives for multi-variable systems, and optimization.
* Integrals: Definite and indefinite integration, finding accumulated totals, and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
* Series and Transforms: Taylor/Fourier series breaking down complex signals into basic frequency components.

    Applications in Electronics Engineering
* Circuit Analysis: Models voltage and current dynamics in capacitors (\(i = C \frac{dv}{dt}\)) and inductors (\(v = L \frac{di}{dt}\)) using differential equations.
* Signal Processing: Uses continuous transformations and Fourier analysis to filter noise and process radio or audio frequencies.
* Control Systems: Evaluates system stability, feedback loops, and transient response times under varying electrical loads.

    Applications in Computer Engineering
* Machine Learning & AI: Powers optimization algorithms like gradient descent to minimize error functions in neural networks.
* Computer Graphics: Drives smooth interpolation, kinematics, lighting simulations, and physical movement in game engines.
* Embedded Systems: Processes real-time sensor data streams and feedback control loops in robotics and IoT hardware.
