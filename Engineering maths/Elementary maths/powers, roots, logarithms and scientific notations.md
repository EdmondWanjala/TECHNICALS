        POWERS, ROOTS, LOGARITHMS AND SCIENTIFIC NOTATION
POWERS AND EXPONENTS;
An exponent indicates how many times a number is multiplied by itself.
    aⁿ = a × a × ... × a (n times).
Laws of exponents:
    aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
    aᵐ/aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
    (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
    (ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ
    a⁰ = 1, where a ≠ 0.
    a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ.

ROOTS AND RADICALS;
A root is the inverse operation of raising a number to a power.
Square root:
    √a = a¹/²
Cube root:
    ∛a = a¹/³
Nth root:
    ⁿ√a = a¹/ⁿ
Important laws:
    √(ab) = √a × √b
    √(a/b) = √a/√b, where b > 0.
    (√a)² = a, for a ≥ 0.
Rationalising the denominator removes radicals from the denominator.
Example:
    1/√2 = √2/2.

LOGARITHMS;
A logarithm determines the exponent to which a base must be raised to obtain a given number.
If:
    aˣ = b
Then:
    logₐb = x.
Conditions:
    a > 0, a ≠ 1 and b > 0.
Laws:
    logₐ(xy) = logₐx + logₐy
    logₐ(x/y) = logₐx - logₐy
    logₐ(xⁿ) = n logₐx
    logₐ1 = 0
    logₐa = 1
Change of base:
    logₐb = log_cb/log_ca.
Common logarithm:
    log₁₀x.
Natural logarithm:
    ln(x) = logₑx.

SCIENTIFIC NOTATION;
Scientific notation expresses very large or very small numbers in the form:
    N = a × 10ⁿ
Where:
    1 ≤ |a| < 10.
Examples:
    300000 = 3 × 10⁵
    0.00045 = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴.
 Applications:
Engineering measurements, electronics, physics, astronomy and scientific calculations.

ENGINEERING PREFIXES;
Tera (T) = 10¹²
Giga (G) = 10⁹
Mega (M) = 10⁶
Kilo (k) = 10³
Base unit = 10⁰
Milli (m) = 10⁻³
Micro (µ) = 10⁻⁶
Nano (n) = 10⁻⁹
Pico (p) = 10⁻¹².

Example:
5 kΩ = 5000 Ω.
10 µF = 0.000010 F.
3.5 MHz = 3,500,000 Hz.

ENGINEERING TIP:
Pay attention to negative exponents and engineering prefixes. Confusing milli (10⁻³) with micro (10⁻⁶), for example, causes significant calculation errors.
