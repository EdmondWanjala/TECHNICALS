        NUMBER SYSTEMS AND BASIC ARITHMETIC
  NUMBER SYSTEMS;
Numbers are classified into different sets:
• Natural numbers (N): Counting numbers, 1, 2, 3, ...
• Whole numbers (W): 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
• Integers (Z): Positive and negative whole numbers, including zero.
• Rational numbers (Q): Numbers expressible as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.
• Irrational numbers: Numbers that cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers, e.g. √2 and π.
• Real numbers (R): All rational and irrational numbers.
• Complex numbers (C): Numbers expressed as a + bi, where i² = -1.
Number set relationship:
    N ⊂ W ⊂ Z ⊂ Q ⊂ R ⊂ C.

BASIC ARITHMETIC OPERATIONS;
Addition (+): Combining quantities.
Subtraction (-): Finding the difference.
Multiplication (×): Repeated addition.
Division (÷): Sharing or determining ratios.

Order of operations (BODMAS):
B – Brackets
O – Orders (powers and roots)
D – Division
M – Multiplication
A – Addition
S – Subtraction

Multiplication and division have equal precedence and are evaluated from left to right. The same applies to addition and subtraction.

FRACTIONS;
A fraction represents part of a whole.
Proper fraction: Numerator < denominator.
Improper fraction: Numerator ≥ denominator.
Mixed number: Whole number combined with a fraction.
Operations:
Addition:
    a/b + c/d = (ad + bc)/bd
Subtraction:
    a/b - c/d = (ad - bc)/bd
Multiplication:
    (a/b)(c/d) = ac/bd
Division:
    (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = ad/bc
Simplification:
Divide numerator and denominator by their highest common factor.

DECIMALS;
Decimals represent fractions using powers of ten.
Examples:
0.5 = 5/10 = 1/2
0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4
0.125 = 125/1000 = 1/8

FACTORS AND MULTIPLES;
Factor: A number that divides another number exactly.
Multiple: A number obtained by multiplying a given number by an integer.
HCF/GCD: Highest common factor of two or more numbers.
LCM: Lowest common multiple of two or more numbers.
Applications:
Simplifying fractions, solving ratio problems and finding common denominators.

PRIME NUMBERS;
A prime number is an integer greater than 1 with exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself.
Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13.
Composite numbers have more than two positive factors.
Prime factorisation expresses a number as a product of prime numbers.
Example:
    60 = 2² × 3 × 5.

ENGINEERING TIP:
Develop accuracy in fractions, decimals, negative numbers and arithmetic operations. Errors in basic calculations can affect complex engineering computations.
