Signal processing is a subfield of electrical engineering and applied mathematics that analyzes, modifies, and synthesizes time- or space-based signals (such as sound, images, and sensor data) to improve transmission, storage, and feature detection. 

Overview;
Signals represent physical phenomena or data streams as a function of time, space, or frequency. Signal processing is divided into two main domains: 
• Analog Signal Processing: Operates directly on continuous-time physical signals using analog hardware like resistors, capacitors, and operational amplifiers. 
• Digital Signal Processing (DSP): Converts analog signals into discrete digital numbers via analog-to-digital converters (ADCs), then processes them using algorithms on microprocessors, DSP chips, or FPGAs. 

Key Topics & Techniques;
• Sampling & Quantization: The process of capturing continuous signals at discrete time intervals (governed by the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem) and mapping amplitudes to finite binary levels. 
• Fourier Analysis & Transforms: Converting signals from the time domain into the frequency domain using the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) or Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) to study frequency content. 
• Digital Filtering: Using mathematical operations (such as convolution) to pass or attenuate specific frequency ranges via Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) or Finite Impulse Response (FIR) filters (e.g., low-pass, high-pass, band-pass). 
• Transforms for System Analysis: Applying the Z-transform for discrete-time systems and the Laplace transform for continuous-time systems to evaluate stability and system response. 
• Time-Frequency & Wavelet Analysis: Analyzing non-stationary signals where frequency content changes dynamically over time. 

Applications;
• Telecommunications & Wireless: Channel modeling, modulation/demodulation, beamforming, and noise reduction for radio, satellite, and cellular networks.
• Audio & Speech Processing: Noise cancellation, automatic speech recognition (ASR), text-to-speech, and audio compression formats like MP3 and AAC.
• Biomedical Engineering: Processing vital physiological signals such as Electrocardiograms (ECGs), Electroencephalograms (EEGs), and medical imaging like MRI and CAT scans.
• Autonomous Systems & Robotics: Fusing and filtering multi-sensor inputs from LiDAR, radar, cameras, and ultrasound for real-time decision-making.
• Geophysics & Seismology: Analyzing seismic waves and structural vibrations to monitor earthquakes and explore subsurface resources.
