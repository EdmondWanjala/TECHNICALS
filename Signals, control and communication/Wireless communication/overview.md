Overview;
Wireless communication is the transfer of information, voice, or data between two or more points without using electrical wires, optical fibers, or physical guided mediums. Instead, it relies on electromagnetic waves (such as radio waves, microwaves, or infrared light), magnetic fields, or acoustic energy traveling through free space. 
A standard wireless system consists of three core components:
• Transmitter: Encrypts, modulates, and amplifies data into an electromagnetic signal.
• Transmission Path (Free Space): The open medium where signals propagate, subject to noise, fading, and interference.
• Receiver: Captures the signal via an antenna, then demodulates, decodes, and restores it to its original data form. 

Key Topics & Technologies;
• Cellular Networks: Spans from early 2G/3G frameworks to modern 4G LTE and 5G cellular systems using hexagonal base stations and mobile switching centers. 
• Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11): High-speed local area networking (LAN) operating typically in the 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz frequency bands. 
• Bluetooth & BLE: Short-range Personal Area Network (PAN) technology designed for low-power device pairing, audio streaming, and wearables. 
• Modulation & Multiplexing: Core techniques (like OFDM, CDMA, and MIMO) that encode digital data onto carrier waves and allow multiple signals to share the same channel. 
• Key Challenges: Signal attenuation, atmospheric interference, multipath fading, limited frequency bandwidth, and over-the-air security vulnerabilities. 

Applications;
• Telecommunications: Mobile cellular phones, voice calls, text messaging, and mobile broadband. 
• Internet & Networking: Home/office Wi-Fi routers, satellite internet for remote regions, and microwave backhaul links. 
• Consumer Electronics & IoT: Wireless headphones, computer mice, keyboards, smart-home sensors, and remote controls. 
• Navigation & Tracking: Global Positioning System (GPS) for real-time positioning and RFID/NFC for asset tracking and contactless payments. 
• Transportation & Emergency Services: Vehicle-to-vehicle (V2X) ad-hoc safety networks, satellite communications for ships/aircraft, and live-video push-to-talk (PTT) systems for disaster response.
