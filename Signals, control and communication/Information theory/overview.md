Information theory is a mathematical study that deals with quantifying, storing, and transmitting data. It was formalized in 1948 by Claude Shannon at Bell Laboratories in his landmark paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication". 
The core philosophy of information theory separates the technical problem of delivering a message from its semantic meaning—focusing strictly on how to package, transmit, and measure information reliably. 

Key Topics;
• Entropy (H(X)): The foundational measure of uncertainty, randomness, or "surprise" in a data source. It dictates the theoretical limits of data compression. 
• Mutual Information (I(X; Y)): A measure that quantifies how much information one random variable shares or reveals about another. 
• Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence: A metric used to measure how one probability distribution differs from a second, reference probability distribution. 
• Source Coding (Compression): Techniques (such as Huffman coding) designed to reduce data size by removing redundancy without losing vital information (lossless) or with controlled loss (lossy). 
• Channel Coding (Error Correction): Methods that add controlled redundancy to detect and correct transmission errors caused by noisy physical channels. 
• Channel Capacity (C): The tight mathematical upper bound on the rate at which reliable data can be transmitted over a noisy communication channel. 

Key Applications;
• Telecommunications & Networking: Underpins modern Wi-Fi, 4G, 5G, and fiber-optic communication systems by maximizing bandwidth and signal-to-noise efficiency.
• Data Compression: Powers everyday file formats and codecs, including ZIP for archives, MP3 for audio, and JPEG for images.
• Cryptography: Provides theoretical foundations for secret-key analysis, randomness extraction, and security bounds.
• Machine Learning & Artificial Intelligence: Guides feature selection, decision trees, and information gain metrics to optimize model learning.
• Neuroscience & Biology: Used to analyze neural spike trains, model sensory processing in the brain, and study DNA sequencing efficiency.
