A control system is an arrangement of physical or computational components that manages, directs, or regulates the behavior of other systems to achieve a desired output state. 

Overview;
At its core, a control system takes an input signal (reference or command), processes it through a controller and a plant (the system being controlled), and produces a desired output. 
Control systems are split into two primary architectures:
• Open-Loop Systems: Operate based on a predetermined input without any feedback or reaction to the actual output. They are simpler and cheaper, but less accurate in the presence of disturbances (e.g., an automatic washing machine timer or toaster). 
• Closed-Loop (Feedback) Systems: Continuously measure the output using sensors and feed that data back to compare it against the desired reference. The difference—known as the error signal—is used by the controller to automatically adjust the system. (e.g., a thermostat or cruise control in a car). 

Key Components & Concepts;
• Sensors: Detect physical parameters (such as temperature, speed, or pressure) and convert them into measurable signals. 
• Controller: Processes the error or state data (often using algorithms like PID—Proportional-Integral-Derivative) to determine the corrective action. 
• Actuators: Execute the physical or electrical adjustment on the system (e.g., motors, valves, relays).

Applications;
Control systems are embedded in modern infrastructure and daily life: 
• Automotive: Anti-lock braking systems (ABS), electronic stability control, and cruise control.
• Aerospace: Aircraft autopilots, satellite attitude control, and drone stabilization (quadcopters).
• Industrial Automation: Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs), robotic assembly arms, and SCADA monitoring systems.
• Building & Home Automation: HVAC climate control, smart thermostats, and automated lighting.
• Energy & Power: Grid load regulation, nuclear power plant safety systems, and turbine speed governors.
