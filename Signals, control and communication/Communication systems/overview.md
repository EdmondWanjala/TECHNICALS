A communication system is an integrated collection of hardware, software, and protocols designed to transmit and receive information from a sender to a receiver across a physical or wireless medium.

Overview & Core Components;
Every communication system follows a fundamental block structure to move data from point A to point B:
• Information Source: Originates the raw message (e.g., voice, text, video, or sensor data).
• Input Transducer: Converts physical or non-electrical data (like sound or light) into an electrical signal.
• Transmitter: Processes, encodes, modulates, and amplifies the signal to prepare it for long-distance travel.
• Communication Channel (Medium): The path through which the signal travels—either wired (twisted-pair, coaxial, fiber optics) or wireless (radio waves, microwaves, satellite).
• Receiver: Captures the incoming signal, amplifies it, demodulates, and decodes it back into a usable format.
• Output Transducer: Converts the electrical signal back into a human-interpretable form (e.g., sound from a speaker or image on a screen).
• Noise & Interference: Unwanted external or internal signals (thermal, shot noise, or atmospheric interference) that degrade signal quality and are measured via Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR).

Key Topics;
 Classification of Signals and Systems
• Analog vs. Digital: Analog systems use continuous-wave signals (varying frequency or amplitude like traditional radio), while digital systems process discrete binary states (1s and 0s like modern computers and fiber optics).
• Modulation Techniques: Methods to superimpose low-frequency baseband signals onto high-frequency carrier waves, including AM (Amplitude Modulation), FM (Frequency Modulation), and PM (Phase Modulation).
• Transmission Modes:
	• Simplex: One-way data flow (e.g., television or radio broadcasting).
	• Half-Duplex: Two-way communication, but not simultaneously (e.g., walkie-talkies).
	• Full-Duplex: Simultaneous two-way communication (e.g., telephone calls).
 Network Topologies and Architecture
• Topologies: Physical or logical layout of a network including Star, Bus, Ring, and Hybrid structures.
• The Communication Framework: Layered models (such as OSI or TCP/IP stacks) splitting tasks into application controls (HTTP, SMTP), routing/addressing (TCP, IP), and physical transmission levels (Ethernet, fiber).
• Security & Error Control: Data protection through encryption, firewalls, authentication, and error-checking protocols (parity checks, checksums, and CRC).

Applications;
• Telecommunications & Cellular Networks: Voice calls, SMS, and high-speed mobile data spanning 4G and 5G infrastructures.
• Broadcasting: Over-the-air and cable distribution of radio, television, and webcasting.
• Satellite & Global Positioning Systems (GPS): Long-distance communications, maritime routing, weather tracking, and global navigation.
• The Internet & IoT (Internet of Things): Global interconnectivity of web services, email, cloud computing, smart homes, and automated machine-to-machine sensors.
• Defense & Emergency Services: Tactical radio networks, radar, satellite phones, and mission-critical dispatch systems.
• Banking & Commercial Systems: Automated Teller Machines (ATMs), electronic funds transfer at point of sale (EFTPOS), and secure online financial transactions.
