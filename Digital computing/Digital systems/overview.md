Digital systems are electronic or physical systems that process, store, and transmit discrete information using binary values (0 and 1, representing low and high voltage or OFF and ON states).

Overview & Core Concepts
• Binary Representation: All data, numbers, text, and instructions are encoded into bits (0s and 1s).
• Logic Gates: The fundamental hardware building blocks (such as AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR) that perform basic logical operations.
• Boolean Algebra & Optimization: The mathematical framework used to analyze and simplify logic circuits via laws, truth tables, and Karnaugh maps.
• Combinational vs. Sequential Circuits:
	• Combinational Circuits: Outputs depend strictly on the current input combination at any given moment (e.g., adders, multiplexers).
	• Sequential Circuits: Outputs depend on current inputs and past history, utilizing clock signals and memory elements like flip-flops and registers (e.g., counters, finite state machines).
• Analog-Digital Interfacing: Uses Analog-to-Digital Converters (ADCs) and Digital-to-Analog Converters (DACs) to bridge real-world continuous signals with discrete digital processing.
• Design & Hardware Description Languages (HDLs): Systems are modeled, synthesized, and verified using industry-standard languages like Verilog and VHDL, often deployed onto FPGAs (Field Programmable Gate Arrays) or custom SoCs (Systems-on-Chip).

Key Topics in Digital Systems
• Number Systems & Codes: Converting between binary, decimal, and hexadecimal, alongside error-detection/correction codes (like Hamming codes) and alphanumeric codes (like ASCII).
• Arithmetic Logic Units (ALUs): Circuits designed to execute binary addition, subtraction, multiplication, and logical comparisons.
• Memory & Storage: Flip-flops, RAM, ROM, and flash memory used to retain binary states over time.
• Microprocessors & Microcontrollers: Central processing units and embedded controllers that fetch, decode, and execute programmed instructions.
• Noise Immunity & Reliability: Why digital transmissions resist signal degradation compared to analog systems through clear voltage thresholds.

Real-World Applications
• Consumer Electronics: Smartphones, computers, smart TVs, and digital audio/video players.
• Telecommunications: 5G/LTE networks, fiber optics, data routing, and time-division multiplexing.
• Automotive Systems: Electronic Control Units (ECUs), anti-lock braking systems (ABS), ADAS/autonomous driving sensors, and EV powertrains.
• Industrial Automation & IoT: Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs), distributed control systems (DCS), and smart factory sensors.
• Healthcare & Medical Devices: Digital imaging (MRI, CT scans), patient monitoring equipment, and diagnostic tools.
