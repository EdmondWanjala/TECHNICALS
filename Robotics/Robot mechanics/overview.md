Robot mechanics is the study of the physical structure, movement, kinematics, and dynamics of robotic systems. 
It bridges mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, and computer science to design machines that interact accurately with their physical environment.

Overview of Robot Mechanics;
At its core, robot mechanics deals with how rigid bodies, joints, links, and actuators assemble to create controlled motion.
• Degrees of Freedom (DoF): The number of independent parameters that define the configuration of a mechanical system (e.g., a rigid body in 3D space has 6 DoF: 3 linear and 3 angular).
• Kinematic Chains: Links connected by joints (such as revolute or prismatic joints) forming open or closed loops.
• Statics and Dynamics: Evaluating forces, torques, inertia, and stress as the robot moves or carries loads.

Key Topics;
• Kinematics: The study of motion without considering the forces that cause it:
	• Forward Kinematics: Calculating the position and orientation of the robot's end-effector from known joint angles or displacements.
	• Inverse Kinematics: Determining the required joint angles to place the end-effector at a desired target position.
• Dynamics: Analyzing the relationship between physical forces, torques, mass, and accelerations using methods like Newton-Euler or Lagrangian formulations.
• Actuators and Transmission: The mechanical and electrical devices that drive movement:
	• DC motors, servo motors, and stepper motors
	• Hydraulic and pneumatic cylinders
	• Gears, belts, and ball screws for torque and speed conversion
• End-Effector Design: The mechanical tools, grippers, or welding torches attached to the end of a manipulator arm to interact directly with objects.

Applications;
Robot mechanics plays a vital role across multiple industries outlined by Michigan Technological University:
• Manufacturing & Assembly: High-speed pick-and-place, welding, painting, and precision component installation.
• Healthcare & Surgery: Surgical robot arms for minimally invasive procedures and automated logistics transport in hospitals.
• Agriculture: Automated harvesting, weed management, and planting systems.
• Logistics & Warehousing: Autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) and articulated sorting arms managing inventory and delivery.
• Construction & Hazardous Environments: Automated drilling, brick-laying, and tele-operated maintenance in extreme conditions.
