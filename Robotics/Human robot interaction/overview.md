Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) is an interdisciplinary field dedicated to understanding, designing, and evaluating robotic systems intended for communication, collaboration, or co-existence with humans. 
Unlike Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)—which focuses primarily on screen-based digital software—HRI deals with physical embodiment, spatial proximity, safety, and real-world dynamics. 

Overview;
• Definition: HRI studies the dynamic exchange of information, physical forces, and social cues between humans and machines.
• Autonomy Spectrum: Interactions range from direct teleoperation (constant human control) and semi-autonomous cooperation to fully autonomous operation.
• Multidisciplinary Roots: It merges artificial intelligence, robotics, psychology, cognitive science, design, and ergonomics. 

Key Topics;
• Interaction & Communication Methods:
	• Verbal: Natural language processing and speech recognition.
	• Non-verbal: Facial expressions, body posture, gestures, and eye gaze tracking.
	• Physical / Haptic: Force-feedback, impedance/admittance control, and safe contact mechanics. 
• Adaptive Behavior & Machine Learning:
	• Algorithms that let robots interpret human intent, adapt to context, and personalize assistance over time. 
• Human-Robot Interfaces & Situational Awareness:
	• Designing control dashboards, map-centric vs. camera-centric views, and clear status/recovery indicators so operators understand what the robot is doing. 
• Safety & Trust:
	• Fail-safe design, collision avoidance, emergency stops, and transparency to build psychological trust in shared spaces. 

Applications;
• Industrial Manufacturing:
	• Collaborative robots (cobots) working side-by-side with human operators on assembly, picking, placing, and welding lines. 
• Healthcare & Assistive Robotics:
	• Surgical robots enhancing precision, hospital navigation assistants (such as PAL Robotics' ARI), and robotic arms or powered exoskeletons helping patients or elderly individuals with daily physical tasks. 
• Service & Hospitality:
	• Public receptionists, customer-service guides, and social companion robots. 
• Field & Extreme Environments:
	• Search-and-rescue units, agriculture/rehabilitative farming, and space or wildland-fire response teams where robots assist human crews under high-stress conditions.
