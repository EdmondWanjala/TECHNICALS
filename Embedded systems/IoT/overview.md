The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to a network of physical objects ("things") embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity technologies that allow them to collect, share, and act on data over the internet without heavy human intervention.

Overview of IoT:
• Core Definition: Connecting everyday physical items (from lightbulbs to industrial machines) to the digital network.
• How It Works:
	1. Perception/Sensors: Gather physical data (temperature, motion, location, or moisture).
	2. Connectivity: Send data via Wi-Fi, Cellular, Bluetooth, Zigbee, or LoRaWAN to the cloud.
	3. Processing & Analytics: Cloud platforms or edge devices analyze the incoming data (using tools like MATLAB or AI).
	4. Action: Devices or dashboards trigger an automated response (e.g., turning on an AC or sending a maintenance alert).
• Basic Architecture: Typically structured into layers such as the Perception Layer, Network Layer, and Application Layer (sometimes expanded to 5 layers including middleware and business logic).

Key Topics & Technologies:
• Sensors and Actuators: Hardware components that measure physical environments and perform physical actions.
• Edge & Mist Computing: Processing data closer to the device (the "edge") to lower latency and save bandwidth rather than sending everything to a distant cloud. Platforms like ThingSpeak or microcontrollers like Arduino and ESP32 help prototype these systems.
• Connectivity Protocols: Lightweight machine-to-machine communication standards like MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, and BLE/Zigbee.
• Data Security & Privacy: Protecting networks from device spoofing, weak passwords, and data breaches, as detailed by the Fortinet IoT Guide.

Major Applications:
• Smart Homes: Automated lighting, smart thermostats, security cameras, and voice-assisted appliances managed via apps.
• Smart Cities: Intelligent street lighting (saving up to 70% energy), smart parking sensors, traffic management, and waste level monitoring.
• Industrial IoT (Industry 4.0): Predictive maintenance of factory machinery, real-time asset tracking, and automated process control.
• Healthcare: Wearable health trackers, remote patient monitoring systems, and connected medical refrigerators.
• Smart Agriculture: Soil moisture sensors, automated irrigation systems, and livestock tracking.
