Computer-Aided Design (CAD) is the use of computer software to create, modify, analyze, and optimize digital 2D and 3D models of physical objects and structures, replacing traditional manual drafting.

Overview & Core Concepts;
• Definition: CAD uses a graphical user interface and geometry kernel to build precise mathematical representations of points, curves, surfaces, and solids.
• Types of CAD:
	• 2D CAD: Flat geometric drawings, blueprints, and floor plans (e.g., AutoCAD LT).
	• 3D CAD: Solid, wireframe, and surface modeling for three-dimensional visualization (e.g., SolidWorks, Autodesk Inventor, CATIA).
	• Parametric CAD: Design-driven by dimensional constraints and functional relationships (e.g., Fusion 360, Onshape).
	• Freeform/Sculptural CAD: Organic shape and mesh modeling (e.g., Rhino, Blender).
• Key File Formats:
	• Native: Vendor-specific formats like .DWG (AutoCAD), .sldprt (SolidWorks), or .CATPart (CATIA).
	• Neutral: Interoperable formats like .STEP, .IGES, .STL, and .DXF for sharing across platforms.

Key Topics & Features;
• Drafting & Documentation: Automated generation of technical drawings, dimensioning, layering, and title blocks.
• Assembly Modeling: Combining multiple individual components into a single virtual product to check fit, clearances, and joints.
• Simulation & Analysis: Built-in tools like Finite Element Analysis (FEA) and motion/stress testing to evaluate how designs behave under real-world loads.
• Manufacturing Integration: Direct translation of digital models into machine instructions for 3D printing, CNC milling, and laser cutting.

Applications Across Industries;
• Architecture & Construction: Designing residential/commercial building floor plans, structural framing, and site layouts (e.g., via Autodesk).
• Mechanical & Aerospace Engineering: Prototyping machine parts, engines, automotive components, and assemblies.
• Civil Engineering: Planning infrastructure such as roads, bridges, drainage systems, and urban layouts.
• Product & Industrial Design: Conceptualizing consumer electronics, appliances, and retail goods.
• Electronics (EDA): Designing printed circuit boards (PCBs) and electrical wiring diagrams.
