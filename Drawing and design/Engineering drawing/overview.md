Engineering drawing is the universal graphic language used by engineers, designers, and technicians to communicate design intent, geometry, dimensions, and manufacturing instructions.

Overview & Core Elements
• Definition: A technical representation combining graphical lines, symbols, and written notes to define a physical part or structure.
• Standards: Governed by international and national standards such as ISO, ASME Y14.5, and BIS to ensure global clarity.
• Title Block: Located typically in the bottom-right corner; contains the part name, drawing number, scale, company logo, author, material, and surface finish.
• Revision History: Located in the top-right corner to track changes and versions over time.
• Alphabet of Lines: Uses standardized line types (thick continuous lines for visible edges, dashed lines for hidden features, and thin center/axis lines) to convey distinct meanings.

Key Topics;
• Projections & Views:
	• Orthographic Projection: Multi-view 2D representations (Front, Top, Side) utilizing First-Angle (ISO) or Third-Angle (ASME) projection.
	• Pictorial Views: Isometric and oblique views providing a 3D perspective of the object.
	• Sectional & Detail Views: Cut-away views revealing internal cavities or enlarged close-ups of complex geometry.
• Dimensioning & Tolerancing:
	• Applying linear, angular, and radial measurements along with Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T) to control form, fit, and function.
• Types of Drawings:
	• Detail/Part Drawings: Focus on a single component with exact dimensions and material specs.
	• Assembly Drawings: Show how multiple components fit together, often paired with a Bill of Materials (BOM).
	• Installation & Schematic Drawings: Map out field placement, wiring, or piping layouts.
• Drafting Tools & CAD: Transitioned from manual tools (T-squares, set squares, compasses, and drafting boards) to modern Computer-Aided Design (CAD) software and Model-Based Definition (MBD).

Applications;
• Mechanical & Manufacturing Engineering: Designing machine parts, tools, engines, and consumer appliances for CNC machining, casting, or 3D printing.
• Civil & Architectural Engineering: Drafting floor plans, structural framing, bridges, dams, and highway layouts.
• Electrical & Electronics Engineering: Creating circuit diagrams, printed circuit board (PCB) layouts, power distribution grids, and solar photovoltaic installation schemes.
• Aerospace & Automotive: Guiding the tight tolerances and complex assembly requirements of aircraft, spacecraft, and vehicle chassis.
