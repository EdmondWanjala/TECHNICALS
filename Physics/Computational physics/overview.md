Computational physics blends physics, applied mathematics, and computer science to solve complex physical problems using numerical analysis and computer simulations.

    Physics Overview
* Definition: It acts as the third pillar of scientific investigation, complementing traditional theoretical and experimental physics.
* Purpose: It tackles non-linear or complicated equations that cannot be solved using exact analytical paper-and-pencil methods.
* Approach: Physical systems are mapped into mathematical models, discretized, and computed iteratively via high-performance algorithms.

    Key Topics
* Numerical Methods: Algorithms like Runge-Kutta for ordinary differential equations, finite difference methods, and Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT).
* Stochastic Processes: Monte Carlo simulations used for random sampling and probabilistic physical states.
* Molecular Dynamics: Simulating the physical movements of atoms and molecules over time.
* High-Performance Computing (HPC): Utilizing parallel processing, clusters, and optimized languages like C, C++, or Fortran to crunch massive datasets.

    Applications in Electronics and Computer Engineering
* Device Modeling & Nanotechnology: Simulating electron transport in nanoscale transistors and semiconductor devices using tools like Density Functional Theory (DFT).
* Integrated Circuit Design: Modeling electromagnetic fields, signal integrity, and thermal dissipation in complex printed circuit boards (PCBs) and microchips.
* Quantum Computing: Designing and simulating fault-tolerant quantum qubits and logic gates before physical fabrication.
* Signal Processing & Dynamics: Applying statistical physics and correlation time analysis to evaluate noise, filtering, and stability in electronic time-series data.
