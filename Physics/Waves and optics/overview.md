Waves and optics provide the foundational physics principles for understanding energy propagation, electromagnetic fields, and light-matter interactions used across modern electronics and computer engineering.

    Physics Overview
* Waves: Oscillatory disturbances that transfer energy through space or a medium without permanently transferring matter. They are classified as mechanical (requiring a physical medium like sound) or electromagnetic (propagating via mutually perpendicular oscillating electric and magnetic fields). Key properties include wavelength (\(\lambda \)), frequency (\(f\)), amplitude, and wave speed (\(v = f\lambda\)).
* Optics: The study of light and its interactions with matter. It splits into geometrical optics (treating light as straight rays for lenses and mirrors via Snell's Law) and wave/physical optics (treating light as an electromagnetic wave to explain phenomena where scale matches wavelength).

    Key Topics
* Superposition and Interference: The net displacement of overlapping waves combining constructively (in phase) or destructively (out of phase).
* Diffraction: The bending of waves around obstacles or apertures comparable to their wavelength.
* Polarization: The restriction of electromagnetic field oscillations to a specific directional plane.
* Electromagnetic Spectrum: The continuous range of all EM frequencies, from long radio waves to visible light and high-energy X-rays.
* Reflection and Refraction: Wave behavior at boundaries between different media characterized by refractive indices (\(n\)).

    Applications in Electronics and Computer Engineering
* Fiber-Optic Communications: Utilizes total internal reflection and wave guidance to transmit high-density digital data over long distances via light pulses.
* Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC) and Signal Integrity: Wave propagation principles govern transmission line theory, impedance matching, and crosstalk reduction on high-speed printed circuit boards (PCBs).
* Optical Interconnects and Computing: Replaces traditional copper wires with light signals inside data centers and multi-core processors to eliminate RC-delay bottlenecks and increase bandwidth.
* Semiconductor Lithography: Extreme ultraviolet (EUV) diffraction and wave interference limits dictate the microscopic resolution used to pattern nanoscale transistors on silicon chips.
* Sensors and Displays: Optoelectronic components—such as photodiodes, lasers, and liquid-crystal polarization filters—rely directly on wave-optics behavior.
