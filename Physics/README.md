                        PHYSICS

This folder contains independent study of physics relevant to
engineering and technology.
Physics for engineers is the bridge between fundamental scientific principles and practical technological innovation, applying core laws of matter and energy to design, analyze, and build systems.
    
    Overview
Physics for engineers applies core laws of matter, motion, and energy to solve practical technical challenges. 
While pure physics focuses on discovering why nature behaves a certain way, engineering physics uses those rules to design, test, and optimize working systems. 
It requires precise measurement standards (like the International System of Units, or SI) and robust mathematical models to ensure structures, circuits, and machines operate safely and efficiently.
    
    Key Topics
* Mechanics and Dynamics: Study of forces, torque, kinematics, and Newton’s laws of motion governing how objects move and interact.
* Work, Energy, and Power: Conservation laws tracking how mechanical or thermal energy converts and transfers within a system.
* Thermodynamics: Principles of heat transfer, temperature, and energy conversion that dictate system efficiency.
* Electromagnetism: Behavior of electric and magnetic fields, circuits, and electromagnetic waves.
* Oscillations and Waves: Simple harmonic motion, resonance, acoustics, and wave optics.
* Modern Physics & Materials: Introduction to solid-state physics, semiconductors, lasers, and fiber-optics.
    
    Real-World Applications
* Mechanical & Automotive Engineering: Using dynamics and thermodynamics to design high-efficiency internal combustion engines, gas turbines, braking mechanisms, and vehicle suspension systems.
* Civil & Structural Engineering: Applying mechanics and material behavior principles to calculate load distribution, ensuring bridges, skyscrapers, and dams withstand structural stress.
* Electrical & Computer Engineering: Leveraging electromagnetism and quantum behavior in solids to build microchips, transistors, semiconductor diodes, wireless communication antennas, and power grids.
* Robotics & Aerospace: Utilizing kinematics for precise computer-vision camera placement, robotic limb trajectory planning, and orbital flight mechanics
