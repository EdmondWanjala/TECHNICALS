Elementary physics is the fundamental natural science that studies matter, motion, energy, and the core forces of the universe across microscopic and macroscopic scales.

Key Topics in Elementary Physics
• Mechanics: The study of motion and forces. It includes kinematics (how things move), dynamics (why things move using Newton’s laws: F = ma), and statics (objects in equilibrium).
• Energy, Work, and Power: The study of kinetic and potential energy, mechanical work (W = Fd), power output, and the law of conservation of energy.
• Heat and Thermodynamics: The study of internal energy, temperature, heat transfer, and the laws governing thermal systems and state changes in matter.
• Waves and Optics: The study of mechanical and electromagnetic waves, sound (acoustics), reflection, refraction, and light behavior.
• Electricity and Magnetism: The study of electrical charges, circuits, Ohm's law (V = IR), magnetic fields, and electromagnetic induction.
• Modern and Atomic Physics: An introduction to the structure of the atom, nuclear reactions, and fundamental particle behavior.

Real-World Applications
• Transportation & Engineering: Newtonian mechanics and aerodynamics govern how automobiles, trains, and aeroplanes travel safely and efficiently.
• Home & Appliances: Thermodynamics and heat transport principles operate inside refrigerators, air conditioners, and cooking tools.
• Communication & Electronics: Electromagnetism and wave mechanics make cell phones, Wi-Fi, radio broadcasting, and touchscreens possible.
• Medicine: Optics, sound waves, and nuclear physics enable tools like ultrasound machines, laser surgery, X-rays, and MRI scans.
• Navigation & Space: Gravitation and kinematics calculations drive GPS satellite systems, weather tracking, and space exploration.
