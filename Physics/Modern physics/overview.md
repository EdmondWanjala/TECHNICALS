Modern physics studies physical phenomena that exceed the limits of classical mechanics and electromagnetism, focusing on atomic scales, high speeds, and quantized energy.

    Overview of Modern Physics
* Definition: Explores the microscopic and subatomic world (quantum mechanics) and extreme velocities near the speed of light (relativity).
* Shift from Classical: Classical physics failed to explain phenomena like blackbody radiation, atomic stability, and the photoelectric effect, leading to 20th-century revolutionary frameworks.
* Core Pillars: Quantum mechanics governs discrete energy states and probabilities, while relativity defines space-time frameworks.

    Key Topics
* Wave-Particle Duality: Entities like light and electrons exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties.
* Energy Quantization: Energy is emitted or absorbed in discrete packets called quanta (E = hf) rather than continuous streams.
* Energy Bands in Solids: Electron interactions in crystal lattices create valence and conduction bands, separating insulators, metals, and semiconductors.
* Tunneling and Probability: Particles can pass through classical potential barriers based on wave function probabilities.

    Applications in Electronics and Computer Engineering
* Semiconductor Devices: Quantum band theory explains silicon doping, allowing the creation of diodes, PN junctions, and transistors that switch billions of times per second inside microprocessors.
* Flash Memory & SSDs: Quantum tunneling enables electron injection and removal in floating-gate transistors used in solid-state non-volatile data storage.
* Optoelectronics & Lasers: Stimulated emission and electron transitions across band gaps drive LEDs, laser diodes, and fiber-optic communication systems.
* Quantum Computing: Utilizes superposition and entanglement of quantum bits (qubits) to process complex logic states beyond binary limits.
