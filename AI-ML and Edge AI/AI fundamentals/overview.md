Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the broad field of computer science focused on building systems and machines that can perform tasks requiring human-like intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, understanding language, and recognizing patterns.

The AI Hierarchy:
• Artificial Intelligence (AI): The overarching science of making machines think and act intelligently.
• Machine Learning (ML): A core subset of AI where computers learn from data and experience rather than explicit, hard-coded rules.
• Deep Learning (DL): A specialized subset of ML using multi-layered artificial neural networks to solve complex problems like speech and face recognition.
• Generative AI (GenAI): Applications built on deep learning (such as Large Language Models) that create new content like text, images, code, and audio.

Key Topics & Core Concepts:
 Machine Learning & Models;
• Supervised vs. Unsupervised Learning: Supervised learning trains models on labeled input-output pairs; unsupervised learning finds hidden patterns or groupings in unlabeled data.
• Training and Inference: Training is the phase where a model learns patterns from massive datasets; inference is when the trained model applies that knowledge to new, real-world data.
• Parameters and Weights: The internal numerical variables of a model that adjust during training to help it make accurate predictions.
 Neural Networks & Deep Learning;
• Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs): Computing systems inspired by biological neurons in the human brain, structured in input, hidden, and output layers.
• Transformers: A deep learning architecture utilizing self-attention mechanisms to process context across long sequences of data (the backbone of modern LLMs).
• Tokens and Embeddings: Tokens are the basic chunks of text or data a model processes; embeddings turn those chunks into numerical vectors that capture semantic meaning.
 Major AI Workloads & Disciplines;
• Natural Language Processing (NLP): Enabling computers to read, understand, translate, and generate human language.
• Computer Vision: Allowing machines to visually interpret, process, and analyze images and video feeds.
• Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): Connecting generative AI models to external knowledge bases or documents to ground their answers in factual data.
• Prompt Engineering: The practice of structuring clear instructions, contexts, and examples to guide an AI model toward more accurate outputs.
 Responsible AI & Ethics;
• Fairness: Ensuring models do not perpetuate or amplify historical biases or discrimination (e.g., in hiring or loans).
• Transparency & Explainability: Making sure how an AI system reaches a decision can be understood by humans (crucial in high-stakes fields like healthcare or finance).
• Privacy & Security: Protecting user data and complying with data privacy regulations like GDPR.
• Accountability: Clarifying who is responsible when an AI system makes an error or causes harm.
