Computer vision (CV) is a field of artificial intelligence (AI) that trains computers to “see,” process, and interpret digital visual data—such as images, video streams, and 3D point clouds—much like the human brain does.

Overview
While human vision relies on eyes and a lifetime of memory and context, computer vision uses digital cameras as "eyes" and deep mathematical and neural models as the "brain".
• Input: Pixels from a camera, scanner, or video feed (structured as numerical grids representing color and intensity).
• Processing: Feature extraction, edge detection, and hierarchical learning via neural networks.
• Output: Semantic understanding, numerical classifications, bounding coordinates, or automated physical actions.

Key Topics & Core Tasks
Computer vision breaks down visual understanding into distinct technical challenges:
• Image Classification: Assigns a single label to an entire image (e.g., "Is there a cat in this photo?").
• Object Detection: Identifies multiple objects in an image and localizes them with bounding boxes and confidence scores (e.g., locating cars and pedestrians on a street).
• Image Segmentation: Provides pixel-level precision by grouping or labeling every single pixel:
	• Semantic Segmentation: Labels all pixels of the same class with the same color (e.g., all road pixels vs. all sidewalk pixels).
	• Instance Segmentation: Differentiates between separate individual objects of the same class (e.g., distinguishing Car A from Car B).
• Pose Estimation: Detects skeletal joint keypoints and body movements (used in motion capture, fitness apps, and gesture control).
• Optical Character Recognition (OCR): Extracts and digitizes text from physical documents, signs, or license plates.
• 3D Reconstruction & Depth Estimation: Builds three-dimensional models or depth maps from 2D images or LiDAR/stereo camera sensors.
• Key Architectures: Driven heavily by Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for spatial hierarchies, Vision Transformers (ViTs) for global context, and real-time detectors like YOLO.

Key Applications;
Computer vision is integrated into everyday technology and heavy industry alike:
• Healthcare: Analyzing X-rays, MRIs, and CT scans to detect tumors, fractures, or early signs of disease.
• Autonomous Vehicles: Helping self-driving cars and drones recognize road signs, lane markings, obstacles, and pedestrians in real time.
• Security & Surveillance: Powering facial recognition for access control, automated license plate reading, and real-time anomaly or threat detection.
• Manufacturing & Quality Control: Inspecting assembly lines at high speeds to spot microscopic defects, rust, or misalignments.
• Agriculture: Monitoring crop health, tracking livestock behavior, and guiding automated spraying or harvesting via drones.
• Retail & E-commerce: Enabling cashierless checkout, visual product search, and in-store customer traffic analysis.
• Augmented & Virtual Reality (AR/VR): Tracking hand gestures, room geometry, and head movement to merge digital overlays with the physical world.
