The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to a network of physical objects ("things") embedded with sensors, software, and processing units that connect and exchange data with other devices and cloud systems over the internet.

Overview & Architecture Layers;
An IoT infrastructure is typically structured into multiple functional layers that handle data from the physical environment to the end user:
• Perception / Edge Layer: Physical sensors and actuators that collect environmental data (temperature, motion, light) or perform mechanical actions.
• Network / Connectivity Layer: Protocols and hardware (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, 5G, LoRaWAN, Zigbee) that transmit raw sensor data.
• Gateway Layer: Devices that aggregate data streams, perform protocol translation, and route information safely.
• Processing / Cloud Layer: Centralized cloud platforms or edge computing nodes that store, filter, and analyze incoming data.
• Application / Presentation Layer: User dashboards, mobile apps, and business logic that turn raw metrics into actionable insights.

Key Topics & Core Components;
• Sensors and Actuators: The eyes, ears, and hands of the system—converting physical metrics to digital signals and vice versa.
• Communication Protocols: Ranging from short-range (BLE, Zigbee) to long-range low-power wide-area networks (LoRaWAN, NB-IoT) and transport/app layers (MQTT, CoAP, HTTP).
• Edge vs. Cloud Computing: Edge computing processes data locally near the device to slash latency, while cloud computing handles heavy machine learning models and massive long-term data storage (e.g., ThingSpeak or enterprise clouds).
• Security & Governance: Device authentication, end-to-end encryption, firmware updates, and zero-trust policies to prevent botnets and data interception.

Major Applications;
• Industrial IoT (IIoT): Predictive maintenance, automated assembly lines, and real-time smart factory asset tracking.
• Smart Cities: Intelligent traffic management, automated street lighting (saving up to 70% energy), smart water meters, and waste bin sensors.
• Logistics & Supply Chain: Fleet tracking, cold-chain container monitoring, and intermodal freight visibility.
• Connected Healthcare: Remote patient monitoring, wearable vital sign trackers, and smart medical refrigerator tracking.
• Smart Homes & Buildings: Automated climate control (smart thermostats), security locks, and lighting integration.
